# Sonic's working character: philosophies, leanings and the record behind them

Started with Jack 2026-10-25. Jack's idea: stances Sonic will defend when it works (coding first), built into a real personality it can pull from, grounded in real past attempts, too much to put in every prompt, so found by meaning like semantic memory.

## The shape (`backend/components/sonic_leanings.py`)
- **Philosophies** (the stable character, about 8): a statement and the reason it holds, both written as true of how the work goes, never as a feeling or a memory. `evidence`, `cause`, `small`, `your_call`, `honest_gaps`, `plain`, `fit_in`, `edges`, `economy`.
- **Leanings** (the many specific habits): each follows from one philosophy, has a `when` description and 3 to 4 short example `asks`, one line in Sonic's voice, and dated, abstract `evidence` notes of the real failures it came from (no text from anyone's messages, no names).
- **Approval:** only `approved: True` entries are ever used. Today three leanings are live (frontend: which rule wins, fix where it renders, read the change back) and three philosophies they depend on (`evidence`, `cause`, `honest_gaps`). Everything else is a draft waiting for Jack: approving is changing one flag.
- **The policy applies in full** (docs/feelings-policy.md): leanings, priorities and ways of working; no emotional stake, no lived experience. A test holds every statement, reason, line and evidence note to the identity check, a feeling-word list and a claimed-memory list. Sonic may say "this has gone wrong in my record" only because an evidence note backs it.

## How a turn uses them (`backend/utils/leanings.py`)
Entries are embedded once (each example ask separately plus the description) and stored in `sonic_leaning_index` with a hash, so a restart re-embeds nothing and an edited entry is re-embedded alone. A tool turn with code tools embeds its request (one embedding call, no model call), scores each entry by its best example, keeps the closest 3 at or above 0.66, and puts them in the prompt under their philosophy, with the philosophy's reason, after the fixed rules so the cached prompt start is unchanged. The person's preferences and the project's conventions always win: a leaning is said once, with the reason, then the person's way is followed.
- **Calibration (real embeddings, 2026-10-25):** requests meant for an entry scored 0.68 to 0.82; the closest miss was 0.63 (an email summary against `say_the_cause`); unrelated requests 0.43 to 0.58. Matching on one long description alone was far blurrier (right and wrong matches overlapped at 0.55 to 0.69), which is why each entry has example asks.
- **Counters** (no text, no person): `leaning_turns`, `leaning_used_<id>` per entry, and the prompt card's "leanings" row shows what they cost.

## The record behind them (evidence)
Each leaning came from something that really happened in Sonic's own recorded turns: the mobile cards bug where it blamed the CSS, a plan whose list missed two cases the code could return, stopping after every plan step to ask what the file answered, twice asking for a path it could have searched for, edit turns that ran out of steps on the read before the diff, a branch name it invented, an approval card it wrote itself, saying it could not edit with a folder connected. The `evidence` notes are the source of "my record shows".

- **Counters, 2026-10-08 to 2026-10-26 (19 days, 1,430 turns):** 79 coding turns, 28 of them with an edit and none tested in the same turn; 10 step-limit exits, 7 of them on 2026-10-22 (7 of that day's 14 coding turns), none on 10-24 or 10-25; 63 follow-ups with 1 pushback; 6 edits applied (5 small, 1 large); 1 plan submitted and approved, 3 execution turns, 4 steps done; the answer checker marked 22 of 371 checked replies as hallucination; 5 turns failed on the usage quota.

## 2026-10-26: what its praised replies have in common (Jack's own history, personal turns excluded)
- **Method.** Every AI reply in Jack's two conversations (2,800), minus any reply within two messages of a personal or crisis keyword (relationships, substances, distress, family, safety, names of people): 1,259 set aside, 1,541 usable. A reply counts as praised when the next message is short and praises or thanks (95 of them). For each of twelve features of a reply, the share of praised replies was compared with the share of all usable replies. Nothing was saved to disk and nothing personal is recorded anywhere; the notes below are about replies' shape only. (A first pass used a filter that matched nothing because of a typo in the script; it was caught and rerun, and no personal text was written down.)
- **What stands out** (the other features were within noise at this sample size): praised replies end on a question much less often (9% against 20%, about 2.7 standard errors), and open with a plain acknowledgment more often (16% against 9%, about 2.3 standard errors). These back two new drafts: `end_when_done` and `acknowledge_then_act` (area `talk`; nothing yet carries leanings into the reply voice, so they are drafts for Jack and for that later step).
- **What did not matter, though one might have expected it to:** length (the medians are identical, 123 words), bullets (praised replies used them slightly less), bold, headings, humor (slightly less in praised ones), emoji, naming the user.
- **Caveats.** One person, 95 praised replies, praise is sparse and tends to follow wins that were already going well, so this describes what went with approval, not what causes it. It must never become a target: the praise rate is not in any prompt or goal (docs/feelings-policy.md, "Why not its own numbers"), and the leanings are evidence of what worked, not something to maximise.

That is the evidence behind the economy philosophy and read_files_together, and it points at the next experiment (a lighter checkpoint nudge, or the plan's pre-read files) more than at a new stance.

## 2026-10-26: do the leanings earn their place? (outcome counters, built)
- **What is counted, per leaning, no text and no person:** `leaning_used_<id>` (put in a tool turn's prompt), `leaning_capped_<id>` (that turn hit its step limit), and for the message after the reply: `leaning_followed_<id>`, `leaning_pushback_<id>` (the same pushback reading as the coding goal: the phrase list or a frustrated read of that message) and `leaning_praise_<id>` (a short message that thanks or praises and does not read as pushback; `code_outcomes.is_praise`, crude on purpose).
- **How it links:** a reply that used leanings is stamped with their ids (`leanings_used`, like the `code_turn` mark), and the next message in that conversation is counted against them. Like the existing coding follow-up counters, the stamp lives in memory, so a message that arrives after a server restart is not counted (it undercounts a little, never overcounts).
- **Seen:** Sonic Health, Turns tab, "Working leanings in use": per leaning the uses, how many got a next message, pushback and praise shares, the step-limit share, and a last row for all coding turns to compare against.
- **How to read it, and not to:** small numbers, and a leaning is used on different work each time, so this is a comparison at most. It is never a target and never in a prompt. What it can honestly say: a leaning that is used often and is followed by pushback more than the baseline is a candidate to reword; one that is never used is a candidate to retire.
